Resin Art Bubbles: Why They Happen and How to Fix Them

Every cause of bubbles in epoxy resin — mixing, temperature, porous inclusions — and the exact fix for each.

Sep 14, 2026

Bubbles are the number one reason beginner resin pieces look amateur. The good news: every bubble has a specific cause, and every cause has a fix. Here’s the full diagnostic.

Cause 1: Stirring Too Fast

Whipping air into the mix is the most common source. Stir slowly and deliberately, like folding batter, for a full 3 minutes. Scrape the sides and bottom of the cup — unmixed streaks cure soft and trap air later.

Fix: Slow down. If you’re already poured, a heat gun pass fixes surface bubbles; deep ones from bad mixing usually can’t be saved.

Cause 2: Cold Resin or Cold Room

Epoxy below ~20°C gets viscous, and viscous resin holds bubbles instead of releasing them. Winter crafting is bubble season.

Fix: Warm sealed resin bottles in ~30°C water for 10 minutes before mixing. Keep the room at 20–25°C during pour and cure. Never microwave resin.

Cause 3: Porous Inclusions

Dried flowers, wood, paper, fabric — anything porous releases trapped air into the resin for hours after pouring. This is the “mystery bubbles that appeared overnight” culprit.

Fix: Seal porous items first with a thin coat of resin or Mod Podge and let it cure fully before embedding. For wood, two seal coats.

Cause 4: Pouring Too Deep

Thick pours generate exothermic heat, which can actually boil the resin and create foam-like bubbles — or worse, crack and yellow the piece.

Fix: Respect the depth rating (usually ~6mm per layer for craft epoxy). Build thickness in layers with 4–6 hours between pours.

The Bubble Removal Toolkit, Ranked

  1. Heat gun — best all-rounder. Quick passes, keep it moving, stay 10–15cm away.
  2. Long-neck lighter / torch — fast and effective on small pieces. Brief passes only; heat yellows resin if you linger.
  3. 91%+ isopropyl alcohol spritz — a light mist pops microbubbles on the surface. Don’t soak it.
  4. Pressure pot — the professional answer (60 PSI crushes bubbles to invisibility), but overkill until you’re selling work.

What Can’t Be Fixed

Bubbles trapped deep in a fully cured piece are permanent. Sanding and recoating fixes surface defects only. When in doubt, the piece becomes a test tile — keep a “fail shelf,” because failed pieces are still useful for testing new pigments.

Bottom Line

Slow stir, warm room, sealed inclusions, thin layers. Get those four right and bubbles drop by 95%.
