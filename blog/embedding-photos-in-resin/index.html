Embedding Photos In Resin: Preserving Memories In Epoxy

Embedding photographs in epoxy resin is one of the most popular ways to preserve cherished memories in a durable, glossy finish. However, not all photos work equally well with resin — the process requires careful preparation and the right materials to achieve professional results. This guide walks you through every step, from photo selection to final sealing.

Photo Selection and Preparation

Not every photograph is suitable for resin embedding. The key considerations are:

  • Print type: Only use laser-printed or inkjet prints on quality paper. Thermal prints (receipts, fax paper) will fade and discolor when exposed to resin chemicals. Digital photos printed on archival-quality paper with pigment-based inks give the best results.

  • Size matters: The photo should be slightly smaller than your resin pour area, with at least a 1/4” border of resin surrounding it. A photo that’s too large can create air pockets or uneven curing.

  • Surface protection: Before embedding, the photo must be sealed. Apply a clear acrylic spray or mod podge to both sides of the print and let it dry completely — typically 2-3 hours. This prevents the resin from dissolving the ink or paper, which would ruin the image.

  • Lamination option: For extra protection, laminate the photo first, then cut it to size. The laminate creates a moisture barrier that’s more reliable than spray alone.

Preparing Your Substrate

The surface you’re pouring onto matters as much as the photo itself:

  1. Seal the substrate: Raw wood or porous materials will absorb resin and cause uneven curing. Apply a thin coat of clear sealer or fast-drying medium, then let dry completely. This is especially important for wood substrates.

  2. Create containment: Build a silicone or hot-glue dam around the edges of your substrate to contain the pour. The dam should be at least 1/2” high for a 1/4” pour. Ensure the dam is smooth on the inside — any texture will transfer to the bottom of your piece.

  3. Level everything: Use a bubble level. Even a 2° tilt will cause resin to pool to one side, potentially obscuring part of the photo. This is the most common cause of embedding failure.

  4. Position the photo: Place the prepared photo face-up in the desired position. You can use small amounts of clear packing tape to temporarily hold it in place, but be careful — tape residue can show through transparent resin.

Mixing and Pouring Resin

Proper mixing is critical — mistakes here are the most common cause of embedding failures:

  • Exact ratio: Use a digital scale to measure resin and hardener at exactly the manufacturer’s specified ratio by weight. The most common ratio is 1:1, but some systems use 2:1 or other proportions. Never guess measurements — even being off by 5% can cause tacky cure or exothermic overheating.

  • Mix slowly: Stir slowly for 3-5 minutes, scraping sides and bottom of the mixing cup thoroughly. Fast stirring introduces bubbles that are difficult to remove later and can create clouds around your embedded photo.

  • Pour technique: Pour the resin slowly and close to the photo surface — no more than 1-2 inches height. Pouring from height creates bubbles that get trapped around the photo edges.

  • First pour thickness: For photo embedding, a 1/8” to 1/4” first layer is ideal. This encapsulates the photo without being so thick that bubbles form around it.

Embedding the Photo

Once the first resin layer is poured:

  • Wait for the right tackiness: The resin should be at a “tacky” stage — no longer fully liquid but not yet set. This typically occurs 10-20 minutes after mixing, depending on temperature (75-85°F optimal). If the resin has already started to cure, it won’t properly encapsulate the photo. If it’s still too liquid, the photo may float or shift.

  • Gently position: Lower the photo into the resin using tweezers or a clean craft stick. Avoid creating air bubbles between the photo and the substrate.

  • Eliminate air bubbles: After the photo is positioned, use a heat gun or torch on low setting, held 2-3 inches away, to gently pass over the surface. Move constantly — don’t linger in one spot or you’ll create localized overheating. This pops surface bubbles and helps the resin flow around the photo edges.

  • Second encapsulation layer: Pour a second thin layer (1/8”) over the photo once the first layer has reached tackiness. This fully encapsulates the photo and prevents edges from lifting later.

Bottom Line

Embedding photos in resin successfully comes down to three fundamentals: proper photo preparation, precise resin mixing, and patient execution. Seal your photo always, measure resin and hardener by weight on a digital scale, and work in a stable 75-85°F environment. The key is waiting for the resin to reach the tacky stage before embedding, then encapsulating with a second layer. Rushing any step — especially mixing or timing — will likely result in visible bubbles, edge lifting, or cloudiness. Display your finished piece away from direct sunlight to prevent yellowing, and enjoy your preserved memory for years to come.

FAQ

Q: Can I embed a photo printed on regular printer paper? A: No — regular inkjet paper will dissolve when it contacts resin, causing the ink to bleed and the paper to disintegrate. Always use laser prints or inkjet prints on archival-quality paper with pigment-based inks, and seal both sides with acrylic spray before embedding.

Q: How do I prevent air bubbles from forming around the photo edges? A: Pour the first resin layer slowly close to the surface, wait for the tacky stage, then gently position the photo. Use a heat gun on low setting passed quickly over the surface to pop any bubbles. Most importantly, pour a second encapsulation layer of at least 1/8” over the photo to fully seal the edges.

Q: Can I embed a photo face-down (image side down) on the substrate? A: Yes, this is the most common method. The image faces the substrate, and a second resin layer encapsulates it. This protects the image surface from scratches and wear. If you want the image visible from the top, embed image-side up and pour a final clear top coat.

Q: How long before I can demold my embedded photo piece? A: Wait at least 24 hours before demolding. For best results, wait 48-72 hours for full cure. The photo-encapsulating layers are structurally sound after 24 hours, but the resin continues to harden for several days.

Q: Will the photo fade over time in resin? A: Pigment-based inks are lightfast and should not fade significantly. However, all resins will yellow slightly over time with UV exposure. Use UV-stable resin and apply a UV-resistant top coat, then display away from direct sunlight for best longevity.

Q: Can I embed multiple photos in one piece? A: Yes — position photos with at least 1/2” of resin between them, and ensure each photo is fully encapsulated with its own layer. Plan the layout before pouring, and consider the visual balance of the composition.

Q: What’s the best resin system for photo embedding? A: A 1:1 mixing ratio casting resin works well for embedding. TotalBoat Sea-Glass Epoxy and System Three SeaCast are popular choices for clear, low-yellowing pours. Avoid fast-cure formulas which give less working time for proper photo positioning.

Q: Do I need a pressure pot for photo embedding? A: No — a pressure pot is only needed for very thick pours (over 1/2”) to prevent exothermic heat buildup. For standard photo embedding pours of 1/4” or less, a pressure pot is unnecessary.

Q: Can I embed a photo in cured resin (encapsulating an already-cured piece)? A: Yes — this is called “top embedding.” Sand the cured surface lightly with 400-grit sandpaper, clean thoroughly, then pour a new resin layer with the embedded photo. The mechanical bond is strong when the surface is properly abraded and cleaned.
