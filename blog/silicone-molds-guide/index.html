Silicone Molds for Resin Art: How to Choose and Care for Them

Types of silicone molds for resin — coasters, trays, jewelry — what to look for, how to make them last, and when to replace them.

Sep 14, 2026

Molds decide the shape of everything you make, and silicone is the only material beginners should consider. Here’s how to buy right and make them last hundreds of pours.

Why Silicone Wins

Epoxy doesn’t bond to silicone, so pieces pop out cleanly with zero mold release. Plastic molds stick, tear, and survive a handful of uses. Silicone molds cost more upfront and pay for themselves by pour ten.

The Shapes Worth Owning First

  • Coaster molds (round + geode edge) — the beginner staple. Buy a 4- or 6-cavity set.
  • Small tray mold — teaches larger pours and edge finishing without committing to furniture.
  • Pendant and earring molds — only if you’re going into jewelry; skip otherwise.
  • Sphere and cube molds — for paperweights and decorative pieces. Harder to demold cleanly, so save these for later.

What Separates Good Molds From Bad

Thickness and finish. Quality molds have thick walls that hold their shape when filled and a glossy interior that transfers shine to your piece. Thin, floppy molds bow outward and give you warped coasters. Matte interiors give matte pieces — fine if that’s the look, but most beginners want glass-shine, which needs glossy mold cavities.

Making Molds Last

  • No heat guns inside molds. Torch the surface from above; blasting the silicone directly degrades it fast.
  • Demold gently. Flex, don’t yank. Tearing starts at sharp corners.
  • Wash with warm soapy water after every few uses. Dried pigment and resin dust transfer texture to the next piece.
  • Store flat, out of sunlight. UV and stacking heavy objects on them warps the cavities permanently.

When to Replace

Cloudy pieces from a previously glossy mold, micro-tears at corners, or cavities that no longer sit flat — retire it. For heavy use, expect 50–100 pours per mold. At a few dollars per mold, that’s fractions of a cent per piece.

Bottom Line

Buy thick, glossy silicone in the three starter shapes, keep heat off the silicone itself, and wash them. Molds are the cheapest long-lived tool in the craft.
