Mica Powder vs Alcohol Ink: Which Resin Colorant to Use

The real differences between mica powders and alcohol inks in epoxy resin — effects, difficulty, cost, and when to use each.

Sep 14, 2026

Color is where resin art gets addictive, and the two main colorants behave completely differently. Picking the wrong one for your project is a classic beginner frustration. Here’s the breakdown.

Mica Powder: The Forgiving One

Mica is a fine mineral powder coated with pigment. Stirred into resin, it gives shimmery, pearlescent, opaque-to-semi-opaque color.

  • Difficulty: easy. Hard to overdo; extra powder just deepens the shimmer.
  • Best for: ocean waves, geode lines, galaxy effects, anything where you want swirls that hold their shape.
  • Watch out for: cheap craft mica can clump. Mix it into a small amount of resin first to make a paste, then stir the paste into the full batch.
  • Cure impact: minimal. Mica is inert and rarely affects curing.

Alcohol Ink: The Wild One

Alcohol ink is a highly concentrated dye. A single drop blooms through resin in organic, translucent petals — the famous petri-dish effect.

  • Difficulty: medium. One drop too many turns the whole piece muddy brown. White ink sinks and creates the petri effect; colors float and spread.
  • Best for: translucent jewelry, petri coasters, tinting clear pours without losing transparency.
  • Watch out for: only use inks made for resin or yupo paper. Marker refills and food coloring contain water or solvents that inhibit curing.
  • Cure impact: real. Keep total ink under ~5% of resin volume or the piece may stay soft.

Can You Combine Them?

Yes, and the best pieces often do: mica for the base swirls, alcohol ink drops on top for translucent blooms, white ink sinker for petri effects. Add mica first, pour, then drop inks while the resin is still fluid.

Cost Comparison

A starter set of 24 mica colors runs $10–15 and lasts months. Alcohol inks cost $3–5 per bottle, and you’ll want 5–8 colors plus white sinker. Start with mica; add inks on project three or four.

Bottom Line

Mica for control and shimmer, alcohol ink for translucent drama. Learn mica first — it’s cheaper and nearly impossible to ruin a pour with.
