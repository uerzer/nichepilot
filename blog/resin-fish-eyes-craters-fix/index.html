Resin Fish Eyes and Craters: Causes and Fix

What resin fish eyes, craters, and dimples are, why they form on contaminated or repoured surfaces, and how to fix and prevent these surface defects

Sep 20, 2026

Resin Fish Eyes and Craters: Causes and Fix

Fish eyes — circular craters or dimples that form in a resin pour — look like the resin refused to wet the surface. They cluster where a drop of something repels the resin, usually oil, silicone, wax, or a release agent, so the liquid beads and curls into a ring around the contaminant. They appear most often when painting over a previous resin coat or a contaminated surface. This guide explains the cause and the fix.

What fish eyes look like

Fish eyes are small round voids, ditches, or craters in the surface of the pour. They may be a collection of shallow dimples or a clear ring-shaped depression. They differ from bubbles, which are trapped air pockets rising to the surface; fish eyes are mostly at or near the surface and are caused by a local failure of the resin to spread and bond.

The contamination that causes them

The most common trigger is a film of a waxy, oily, or silicone-based substance on the surface you poured over. Common culprits:

  • Silicone mold release left on a repour or on tools that touch the surface.
  • Finger oil from handling the workpiece.
  • A waxy coating on cardboard, plastic, or coated wood.
  • Pollen, dust, or a thin dust film that makes part of the surface non-wetting.
  • Residue from a previous polish, wax, or oil finish.

Because these contaminants repel the resin, the liquid pulls back, leaving the crater.

How to fix fish eyes

Sand the affected surface to break the contaminant film and expose clean resin. Sand through 220 to 400 grit until the work is dull and evenly scratched, clean with isopropyl alcohol on a lint-free cloth, let it fully dry, then pour a thin fresh coat. The cover hides the craters and bonds to the freshly abraded surface. If only a small area is affected, spot-sanding that region is enough before the topcoat.

Prevention

  • Do not use silicone-based releases on surfaces you will repour; skim the mixing cup instead or tape over it.
  • Wipe the workpiece with isopropyl alcohol before pouring and let it dry completely.
  • Handle pieces by the edges to avoid finger oil on the pour surface.
  • Sand and clean any repour before the fresh coat, even if it looks glossy.
  • Porous surfaces must be sealed; a waxy handler’s glove or oil from the hands transfers easily.

Bottom Line

Fish eyes and craters come from a local film of oil, silicone, or wax that repels the fresh resin. Fix them by sanding the surface to break the contaminant and expose clean resin, cleaning with alcohol, and pouring a thin fresh coat. Prevent them by keeping oils and releases off the pour surface and by sanding and cleaning every repour before recoating.

FAQ

Q: What causes craters in resin? A: Craters (fish eyes) are caused by a local film of oil, silicone, or wax on the surface that repels the resin, pulling it into a ring. They are not mixing errors.

Q: Can I fix fish eyes without re-pouring the whole piece? A: Yes. Sand the affected area through about 400 grit, clean with isopropyl alcohol, and pour a thin fresh coat over it.

Q: Why do fish eyes appear when I repour over cured resin? A: Cured or contaminated surfaces hold oils and release agents that repel fresh resin. Sand and clean the surface before any repour.

Q: Are fish eyes the same as bubbles? A: No. Bubbles are trapped air rising to the surface; fish eyes are craters from surface contamination that makes the resin refuse to wet the area.

Q: Does a mold release cause fish eyes in resin? A: Yes. Silicone mold release left on a surface repels fresh resin and produces fish eyes. Use sparingly and clean thoroughly before repouring.

Q: Can I just pour another layer over fish eyes? A: Only after sanding and cleaning. Poured directly over the craters, a fresh layer reproduces the defect because the contaminant remains.
