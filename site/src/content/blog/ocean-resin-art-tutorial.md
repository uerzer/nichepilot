Ocean waves are the most-requested resin effect and the most commonly botched. Milky smears instead of crisp lacing almost always come down to timing and pigment choice. Here's the method that works.

## Supplies

- Serving board, canvas panel, or MDF round as your base
- Epoxy resin (medium viscosity — runny resin won't hold cells)
- Pigments: deep blue, turquoise, sandy beige for shore, plus opaque white specifically made for waves
- Heat gun (a hair dryer on high, no heat, is a weak substitute — real heat matters)
- Painter's tape for the back and edges

## The White Pigment Matters Most

Not all whites make waves. You need a dense, opaque white pigment — dedicated "wave white" pastes outperform generic white mica, which goes translucent and disappears. This single choice explains most failed oceans.

## The Process

1. **Tape the back** of your piece. Resin drips over edges; tape lets you peel them off cleanly later.
2. **Sand and seal** raw wood with a thin clear coat first, or air from the grain will bubble through your ocean all night.
3. **Pour the water.** Mix blue and turquoise batches, pour in bands from dark (deep water) to light (shore). Tilt to blend the transitions.
4. **Pour the sand.** Beige batch at the shore end. Slight overlap with the lightest blue.
5. **Wait 10–15 minutes.** This is the step everyone skips. The resin needs to thicken slightly or the white sinks straight through.
6. **Lay the white line** where water meets sand — a clean opaque band.
7. **Blow it back** with the heat gun held low (nearly parallel to the surface), pushing the white back into the blue in one smooth pass. The shearing creates the cells and lacing. One pass per section — reworking kills the cells.
8. **Torch bubbles, cover, cure 24 hours.** Pick off dust with tweezers in the first hour if needed.

## Troubleshooting

- **White sank and vanished** → poured too early, or white too thin. Wait longer next time.
- **No cells, just a smooth blend** → heat gun too high or too slow. Low angle, quick pass.
- **Muddy colors** → overworked. Ocean is a two-pass technique; put the gun down after the second pass.
- **Bubbles overnight** → unsealed base. Seal porous surfaces, always.

## Bottom Line

Opaque wave white, a 15-minute wait, one low fast heat-gun pass. That sequence is 90% of realistic waves.
