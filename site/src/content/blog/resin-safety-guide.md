Resin safety advice online splits into "it's basically water" and "you'll die." Both are wrong. Here's the evidence-based middle: real risks, proportional precautions.

## The Actual Risks

**Skin sensitization (the big one).** Uncured epoxy can trigger an allergic reaction that gets worse with each exposure — and once developed, it's permanent. This is the risk that ends crafting careers. It comes from repeated bare-skin contact, not one accident.

**Vapor irritation.** Epoxy vapors irritate eyes, nose, and throat, especially with large pours in small rooms. Most craft epoxies are low-VOC, but "low" isn't "zero."

**Dust from sanding.** Cured resin dust is a mechanical irritant to lungs. Sanding without a mask is the most common safety failure among intermediate crafters who outgrew beginner caution.

## The Gear That Covers All Three

- **Nitrile gloves, changed often.** Not latex (resin penetrates it). Change the moment they feel tacky inside — sweat plus resin against skin is exactly the sensitization pathway.
- **Respirator with organic vapor cartridges + P95/P100 prefilters.** One device covers vapors while pouring and dust while sanding. The $30–40 half-face models from 3M or equivalent are the standard. Dust masks cover dust only — fine for sanding, useless for vapors.
- **Safety glasses.** Splashes happen when mixing vigorously or cutting cured pieces.
- **Ventilation.** A window plus a fan moving air across your workspace and out. For pours over ~500ml or daily crafting, step up to active exhaust (a bathroom-style fan venting outside).

## The Rules

1. Gloves on before bottles open, off after tools are down. No "just this one quick pour."
2. Long sleeves or barrier cream on forearms — sensitization happens there too.
3. Never eat, drink, or touch your face at the resin table.
4. Keep resin and hardener away from kids and pets, locked if possible. Curious hands plus sticky bottles is a bad combo.
5. Dispose of contaminated cups and gloves in a sealed bag — curing leftovers can heat up in trash.

## What About "Non-Toxic" Labels?

"Non-toxic" on craft resin refers to the cured state and specific test standards — it never means the uncured liquids are skin-safe. Treat every resin with the same precautions regardless of marketing.

## Bottom Line

Gloves always, respirator for big pours and all sanding, ventilate the room. Total cost ~$60, and it protects the only hands you've got.
