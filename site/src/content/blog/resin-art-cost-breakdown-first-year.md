"Resin art is expensive" is half true. The startup is cheap; the drip-feed of consumables is where money quietly goes. Here's the full first-year math so nothing surprises you.

## Month 0: Startup ($100–170)

The minimum viable setup, from our [supplies list](../resin-art-supplies-list/):

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| 1:1 epoxy kit, 500ml–1L with molds and tools | $30–60 |
| Respirator with vapor cartridges | $30–40 |
| Box of nitrile gloves (100) | $12–15 |
| Safety glasses + drop cloths | $10–15 |
| Mica starter set (if not in kit) | $10–15 |
| **Total** | **~$100–170** |

This makes your first 10–15 pieces. Everything after this is consumables and upgrades.

## Monthly Consumables ($25–50/month at hobby pace)

Resin itself dominates. A weekly pourer uses roughly 1–2L of epoxy a month ($30–60/L in small bottles — buying gallons drops this 30–40%). Gloves go at 4–8 pairs per session; a 100-box lasts 2–3 months ($5/month amortized). Mixing cups are reusable if silicone ($0 after the first set) or a steady $5–8/month if disposable plastic. Pigments last months at beginner volumes.

Realistic hobby burn: **$25–50/month**. Daily pourers double it.

## The Upgrade Ladder (Buy in This Order)

1. **Heat gun ($25–35)** — month 2–3, when a lighter starts feeling slow. Better bubbles, faster workflow.
2. **Digital scale 0.1g ($12–18)** — when you move to 2:1 resins or want repeatable custom colors.
3. **Wet/dry sandpaper + polishing compound ($20–30)** — when edge finishing becomes the bottleneck to pieces you're proud of.
4. **Pressure pot + compressor ($200–300)** — only when bubble-free clarity directly earns money (selling). Never before.
5. **UV resin + lamp ($30–50)** — only when starting jewelry specifically. It's a second craft with its own supply chain; see our [UV resin jewelry guide](../uv-resin-jewelry-beginners/).

Total sensible year-one spend: **$400–700** including startup. Anyone spending more is collecting gear, not making art.

## [AFFILIATE: beginner epoxy kits] — What to Buy

When comparing kits, price per milliliter of resin is the only number that matters — ignore mold counts and color counts in the headline. A $45 kit with 1L of resin beats a $40 kit with 500ml every time, since resin is 70%+ of your ongoing cost.

## When It Starts Paying for Itself

The math per piece: a set of 4 coasters costs ~$3–5 in materials and sells for $20–35 at craft markets. That's roughly $15–25 profit per set after fees.

- **Casual selling** (2 sets/month): covers all consumables by month 4–5. The hobby becomes free.
- **Serious side income** (10 sets/month): ~$200/month profit, full payback of year-one spend around month 6–8.
- **What kills the math:** underpricing ("but materials were only $4"), giving pieces away, and upgrading tools before sales justify them.

Pricing detail lives in our [how to start guide's](../how-to-start-resin-art/) progression path — sell coasters first, fund everything else with the profits.

## The 3 Money Mistakes

**1. Buying the pressure pot in month one.** A $250 tool solving a problem your $30 heat gun handles at 90% quality. Buy tools with profits, not savings.

**2. Bulk-buying resin before you know your pace.** Opened resin keeps 6–12 months. A gallon that half-cures in the bottle is money in the trash. Scale bottle size to actual monthly use.

**3. Counting your time at $0.** At 2 hours per coaster set netting $15, you're at $7.50/hour. Fine for a hobby, terrible as a business — raise prices or speed up before calling it income.

## FAQ

**Is resin art an expensive hobby?**
Mid-range. Cheaper than photography, pricier than drawing. $100–170 starts you, $25–50/month sustains you — comparable to a gym membership that produces coasters.

**How can I cut resin costs?**
Buy resin by the gallon once you exceed 2L/month, reuse silicone cups, and cure every drop — keep spare small molds handy for leftover mix instead of tossing it.

**Do I need the expensive brands?**
No. Mid-price craft epoxies ($35–50/L) perform within a few percent of premium ones for art pieces. Pay for volume and fresh stock (check manufacture dates), not branding. Our [kit guide](../best-resin-art-kit-beginners/) breaks down what actually differs.

**When should I buy a pressure pot?**
When unclear pieces are visibly costing you sales — typically after 30+ pieces sold, never before.

## Bottom Line

$100–170 to start, ~$35/month to run, $400–700 all-in for year one. Sell a few coaster sets and the hobby funds itself; the only real waste is gear bought before skill demands it.
