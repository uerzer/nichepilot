Starting resin art is simple, but buying your first kit is where most beginners go wrong. They either overspend on professional gear they don't need, or buy a suspiciously cheap kit that cures cloudy and yellows in a month. This guide covers exactly what to look for.

## Epoxy vs. UV Resin: Pick One

For beginners, this is the first decision:

- **Epoxy resin (two-part)** — Best for coasters, trays, river tables, and anything larger than a coin. Cures in 24–72 hours. More forgiving with pigments and inclusions.
- **UV resin (one-part)** — Best for small jewelry pieces. Cures in minutes under a UV lamp, but only in thin layers. Useless for deep pours.

If you're just starting out, get a **two-part epoxy kit**. It teaches you the fundamentals — mixing ratios, bubble removal, cure times — that transfer to every other resin technique.

## What a Good Beginner Kit Includes

A kit worth buying contains:

1. **Resin + hardener in 1:1 ratio** — 1:1 is far easier to measure than 2:1 for a first-timer.
2. **Silicone molds** — at least a coaster set. Silicone releases cleanly; avoid plastic molds.
3. **Pigments or mica powders** — 3–5 colors is plenty to start.
4. **Mixing cups, sticks, and gloves** — non-negotiable. Buy extra cups; you'll use more than you think.
5. **A heat gun or torch** — for popping surface bubbles. A long-neck lighter works in a pinch.

## Safety Gear Is Not Optional

Uncured epoxy resin is a sensitizer: repeated skin contact can develop into a permanent allergy. Minimum setup:

- Nitrile gloves (not latex — resin eats through latex)
- A respirator with organic vapor cartridges when pouring anything over ~200ml
- Ventilation — a cracked window plus a fan beats a sealed craft room

If a kit doesn't remind you about safety in its instructions, that's a red flag about the seller.

## The 3 Beginner Mistakes

**1. Wrong mixing ratio.** Measure by volume (for 1:1) with marked cups, stir slowly for 3 full minutes, scraping the sides. Under-mixed resin stays tacky forever.

**2. Pouring too deep.** Standard craft epoxy handles ~6mm per layer. Deep pours overheat, crack, or yellow. Build thickness in layers, waiting 4–6 hours between them.

**3. Working in the cold.** Resin below ~20°C cures slowly and traps microbubbles. Warm the bottles in a water bath (sealed, ~30°C) for 10 minutes before mixing in winter.

## How Much to Spend

A solid beginner epoxy kit (500ml–1L resin plus molds and tools) runs **$30–60**. Below $25 you're gambling on resin quality; above $80 you're paying for branding or volume you can't use before the shelf life runs out (opened resin keeps ~6–12 months).

## Bottom Line

Buy a 1:1 epoxy kit with silicone molds, wear nitrile gloves, measure carefully, and keep pours thin. Your first coasters won't be perfect — mine had more bubbles than a soda — but the fundamentals click fast, usually by project three or four.
