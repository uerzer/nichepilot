# Cloudy Resin: Causes and How to Fix It

Cloudy resin — a milky, frosted, or hazy appearance in what should be a clear pour — is one of the most common resin disappointments. Unlike yellowing, cloudiness is frequently fixable because it is usually a surface or moisture problem, not a degradation of the polymer. This guide breaks down each cause and the exact remedy.

## Moisture and humidity: the main culprit

The leading cause of cloudy resin is water in the mix or on the surface. Epoxy and water do not mix; even trace moisture emulsifies into microdroplets that scatter light, producing a milky haze. Moisture gets in through:

- Resin or hardener containers left open in a humid room.
- Humid air (above about 60% relative humidity) reacting at the surface during the early cure.
- Wood or paper substrates that were not fully dried before pouring.
- Tools, cups, or mixing sticks that were damp.

The fix depends on whether the cloudiness is on the surface or throughout the pour. If it is a surface haze that appears only at certain angles, a light sand and a fresh thin coat of resin restores clarity. If the milkiness is deep in the pour, sanding will not help — the moisture is trapped in the polymer.

## Off-ratio mix

Mixing resin and hardener at the wrong ratio leaves unreacted resin or hardener in the cured piece, and the excess component creates a milky, oily, or tacky appearance. The hardener-rich excess is the more common culprit and often shows as a cloudy waxy film. Prevent this by weighing your ratio with a scale calibrated to 0.1 g and stirring the full recommended time, scraping the sides and bottom of the cup.

## Bubbles that never rose

Thousands of micro-bubbles suspended in a thick pour scatter light and look like cloudiness even though the surface is glassy. A heat gun or torch pass shortly after the pour pops surface bubbles, and a clear, level cure environment lets deeper bubbles rise. If a cured piece is bubble-cloudy, sand the surface and pour a thin clear cap to flood over it — but trapped deep bubbles are hard to remove after cure.

## Incomplete cure from cold

A pour that cured in a cold room below the resin's minimum temperature may finish soft, tacky, and hazy because cross-linking never completed. Move the piece to a warm (70–80°F) location and allow extended cure time. If the surface is sticky or soft after 72 hours, sand and recoat, which usually restores clarity once the fresh coat cures properly.

## Which cloudy = which fix

| Cloudy type | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Surface haze, angle-dependent | Clear straight-on, milky at glancing angles | Wipe water droplets; sand 220→400 grit; thin clear topcoat |
| Deep milky white | Milk throughout the pour | Moisture in mix; not fixable — re-pour with dry materials |
| Oily/waxy film | Dull waxy look with tack | Off-ratio mix; sand and recoat at correct ratio |
| Micro-bubble fog | Tiny pits under a clear surface | Pop with torch next time; thin cap coat now |
| Soft hazy after 48h | Cloudy + marshmallow-firm | Cold cure; warm it or recoat |

## Prevention checklist

Resin, hardener, cups, and tools must be dry. Pour in a room under 60% relative humidity — a dehumidifier helps in humid climates. Dry porous substrates (wood, cardboard, paper) fully. Stir the exact ratio for the full recommended time. Pop surface bubbles with a torch pass right after pouring. Keep the pour at the middle of the recommended temperature range.

## Bottom Line

Cloudy resin is usually moisture, an off-ratio mix, unpopped micro-bubbles, or incomplete cold cure — not irreversible damage. Diagnose whether the haze is on the surface or throughout the pour. Surface haze sands out and recovers under a thin fresh coat; deep milkiness means trapped moisture and requires a clean re-pour. Keeping materials dry and hitting the ratio are the two fixes that prevent most cloudiness.

## FAQ

**Q: Can cloudy resin be fixed, or do I need to re-pour?**
A: Surface cloudiness fixes with a light sand and a thin clear topcoat. Cloudiness sunk deep in the pour cannot be removed and needs a re-pour with dry materials and the correct ratio.

**Q: What causes a milky white resin piece?**
A: Trapped moisture in the mix is the most common cause, followed by an off-ratio hardener excess. Water emulsifies into droplets that scatter light, producing the milk.

**Q: Does sanding fix cloudy resin?**
A: Only surface haze. Sand from 220 to 400 grit, clean, and apply a thin fresh coat to restore a clear glossy surface. Deep haze does not respond to sanding.

**Q: Will a heat gun remove cloudiness?**
A: Not after cure. A torch only prevents bubbles before they set. Once the pour has cured cloudy, heat will not restore clarity.

**Q: Can humidity alone make a pour cloudy?**
A: Yes, high humidity during cure reacts at the surface and can produce a hazy finish even with a perfectly measured mix. Dehumidify the room for the full cure.

**Q: Is cloudy resin safe to use?**
A: Generally yes if it is fully hardened. Cloudiness is a cosmetic defect, not a structural one. It is safe but looks bad.
